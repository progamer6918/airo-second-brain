# Source Dossier - ASSDP Advance Tanadi Santoso Material

Source: `ASSDP Advance Tanadi Santoso Material.pdf`  
Units: 104 pages  
Authority: customer, organizational-development and change-leadership training reference  
Currentness: historical 2025 training method.

## 1. Focus on customer - pp.1-32

The source starts with the Business Model Canvas and pulls attention toward the customer side: value proposition, relationship, segment and channel.

### Three tensions of customer centricity - p.6

Customer centricity is not “always say yes.” It manages tensions:
- **Stability vs Change** - customer wants consistent service but also innovation.
- **Openness vs Privacy** - customer wants transparency while personal data remains protected.
- **Close vs Professional** - customer wants warmth/familiarity with professional boundaries.

### Three foundations of trust - pp.8-18

1. **Empathy** - understand emotion and need.
2. **Effective Communication** - clear, concise, relevant, two-way; verify understanding and use the right medium.
3. **Psychological Safety** - people feel safe to speak, admit mistakes and contribute ideas.

Psychological safety is framed as a learning/invention condition, not merely “being nice.”

### Satisfaction / NPS / needs - pp.23-32

NPS formula:
`NPS = % Promoter - % Detractor`.

Promoter 9-10, Passive 7-8, Detractor 0-6.

The deck gives generalized interpretation bands (-100 to 0, 0-30, 30-60, >60). Treat them as training interpretation, not Honda contractual thresholds.

Customer needs are framed as:
- emotional;
- business/rational.

CASH expectation mnemonic:
- Convenience;
- Action;
- Speed;
- Hassle Free.

## 2. Power of Moments - pp.33-51

The source reframes experience design around memorable moments rather than averages.

Moment types shown:
- transitions;
- milestones;
- pits.

Four “power moments” dimensions:
- **Elevation** - rise above ordinary routine.
- **Insight** - compressed realization / change of perspective.
- **Pride** - recognition/accomplishment.
- **Connection** - shared meaning and relationship.

Operational interpretation: customer experience can be deliberately designed at specific moments such as delivery, service waiting, problem recovery, achievement and community interaction.

## 3. Organizational development / culture - pp.53-69

The source separates:
- espoused values;
- observed behavior.

A culture gap exists when stated values and actual behavior diverge.

Failure modes of bureaucracy listed include bloat, friction, distortion, apathy, rigidity, conformity, inward focus, fear and politics.

Humanocracy principles shown:
- Ownership;
- Meritocracy;
- Markets;
- Community;
- Openness;
- Experimentation;
- Balance.

The travel-approval example uses a controlled experiment: hypothesis -> selected treatment/control group -> measures -> time-bounded test. This reinforces an important WorkDesk rule: **organization change can be tested, not merely announced**.

## 4. AI as a coaching amplifier - pp.70-79

The deck frames AI as a tool to make people smarter. Prompting guidance:
- be precise;
- provide context/background;
- iterate/refine;
- request examples;
- test different approaches;
- use empathy/natural language.

This is a durable interaction pattern, not a model/version policy.

## 5. Leading change - pp.81-100

### Strategy-to-performance gap - p.83

Change needs all five:
`Vision + Skills + Incentives + Resources + Action Plan`.

Missing element -> failure mode:
- missing Vision -> Confusion;
- missing Skills -> Anxiety;
- missing Incentives -> Gradual Change;
- missing Resources -> Frustration;
- missing Action Plan -> Not Implemented.

### Organizational hurdles - p.86

- Cognitive / understanding hurdle.
- Resource hurdle.
- Motivational hurdle.
- Political hurdle.

### Strategic posture - p.87

The source contrasts Holder / Reactor / Shaper. The reusable lesson is posture toward change: defend status quo, react to external change, or deliberately shape the environment.

### Change actions - pp.88-99

Themes include:
- create urgency;
- balance care with demanding expectations;
- trust / capability / importance messages;
- tell stories;
- tweak the environment;
- create short-term wins;
- shrink the change into smaller steps;
- use a “15% principle” as influence/leadership metaphor;
- culture embedding progression: **Paksa -> Bisa -> Biasa -> Budaya**.

## Guard

Many cases are external (hotel, Grab, organizational examples). Use them as analogies and mechanisms, not as direct evidence that the same intervention will work in Honda dealer operations.
