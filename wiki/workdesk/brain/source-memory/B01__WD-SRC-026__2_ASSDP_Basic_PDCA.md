## What this source is actually for

This deck does two jobs. First, it introduces PDCA as a continuous management loop. Second, and more importantly for WorkDesk, it maps PDCA onto the work of an **Area Supervisor**. The practical value is therefore not merely the definition of Plan–Do–Check–Action; it is an operating rhythm for supervising market, dealer, sales, stock, programs, people, channel, financial-company coordination, field intelligence, dealer review, PICA, and corrective action.

The deck's implicit management model is: **forecast and set direction → execute and observe → compare actual vs expected and diagnose → correct/improve → repeat consistently.**

## Concept map

`PDCA` connects the following work objects:

- **Plan** → market forecast, market share, sales per type, target setting, next strategy/activity, dealer supervision planning.
- **Do** → daily sales/stock monitoring, program/activity monitoring, competitor monitoring, ensuring AHM/MD policy execution, field supervision, collaboration with Honda network and financial companies.
- **Check** → external-driver impact analysis, achievement monitoring, market-share evaluation, dealer review, supervision of strategy execution, discussion of next action.
- **Action** → PICA, dealer improvement, strategy/activity/program proposals, coordination with financial companies and AHM, field-information feedback to strengthen analysis.

This is a **closed loop**, not four unrelated task buckets.

## Slide-by-slide semantic map

### Slide 1 — Title / scope
Introduces `ASSDP Basic — PDCA`, from PT Astra Honda Motor. No substantive operating rule beyond identifying the topic and institutional context.

### Slide 2 — PDCA definition
The deck defines:

- **Plan**: establish objectives and processes needed to deliver expected output/targets/goals.
- **Do**: implement the plan and execute the process; collect data for charting and analysis.
- **Check**: compare actual DO results with expected PLAN results to identify differences/deviations and assess appropriateness of execution.
- **Action**: request corrective action between actual and planned results; analyze differences to determine root causes.

Visual semantics: a circular four-stage PDCA diagram communicates iteration/continuity rather than a one-time linear project.

### Slide 3 — Translation to role
The slide asks: **“What do we do as AREA SUPERVISOR?”** This is the pivot from generic PDCA theory to role-specific operations.

### Slide 4 — PLAN for Area Supervisor
Explicit activities:

1. Forecast market based on market, market share, and down to sales per type, then determine targets consistently.
2. Routinely coordinate with AHM and Dealer to determine monthly/yearly targets and next strategy/activity for performance and development, including examples such as people, channel, and digitalization.
3. Plan routine supervision activities to Dealer.

Operational interpretation: planning is not limited to a numeric target. It combines **market view + target + strategy/activity + supervision plan**.

### Slide 5 — DO for Area Supervisor
Explicit activities:

1. Daily monitoring of sales and stock conditions at Dealer.
2. Monitoring and documentation of Honda and competitor programs/activities.
3. Ensure AHM and Main Dealer policies/strategies are executed by Dealer.
4. Conduct routine supervision to Area and Dealer.
5. Routinely coordinate/collaborate with Honda network and financial companies to understand sales conditions and field information.

Diagnostic implication: execution evidence includes both **quantitative performance** and **field/competitive observations**.

### Slide 6 — CHECK for Area Supervisor
Explicit activities:

1. Analyze/evaluate impact of economic, political, social, and environmental conditions on the local market.
2. Monitor daily/weekly/monthly achievement against target and evaluate market-share achievement.
3. Conduct Dealer Review across market, sales, stock, sales people, cash & credit, plus supervision to ensure strategy runs properly.
4. Discuss next actions based on performance evaluation of strategies already executed, at daily/weekly/monthly cadence.

This makes `CHECK` materially richer than “look at KPI.” It includes external context, target comparison, market share, a multi-domain dealer review, strategy-execution verification, and decision discussion.

### Slide 7 — ACTION for Area Supervisor
Explicit activities:

1. Build improvements from Retail and M/S achievement evaluation using PICA, based on Area market and competitor information.
2. Improve Dealer based on field-supervision and Dealer Review results.
3. Propose strategy, activity, and program from field evaluation for Area/Dealer together with financial companies and AHM.
4. Routinely update AHM with field information and achievement related to market, competition, and Honda network conditions to strengthen PICA analysis.

The Action stage therefore contains both **corrective action** and **upward feedback / coordination**.

## Conceptual memory to preserve

1. **PDCA is the supervisory operating loop.**
2. Planning combines market forecast, target, strategy/activity, and supervision plan.
3. Doing includes monitoring, execution control, documentation, field supervision, and network/fincoy coordination.
4. Checking means compare expected vs actual **and diagnose why**, including external drivers and dealer operating dimensions.
5. Action means corrective improvement/PICA plus proposal/coordination and feedback to AHM.
6. Dealer Review is a central `CHECK` mechanism; PICA is a central `ACTION` mechanism.
7. The loop is expected to operate at multiple cadences (daily, weekly, monthly; target planning also monthly/yearly).

## Procedural memory

A source-grounded supervisory sequence is:

1. Forecast the market and expected sales down to type level.
2. Determine targets with AHM/Dealer and define next strategy/activity.
3. Plan supervision.
4. Execute/monitor sales and stock daily.
5. Monitor/document Honda and competitor activity.
6. Verify dealer execution of AHM/MD strategy and supervise the field.
7. Collect field information through network/fincoy collaboration.
8. Compare actual achievement and market share with target/plan.
9. Run Dealer Review across market, sales, stock, sales people, cash & credit.
10. Evaluate whether strategy was actually executed and what happened.
11. Analyze relevant external conditions.
12. Build PICA/corrective improvements and propose next strategy/activity/program.
13. Feed field evidence back to AHM and continue the next PDCA cycle.

This sequence is a faithful compilation of slides 4–8. Exact workflow dependencies beyond what is stated are not asserted as mandatory policy.

## Diagnostic memory

The deck explicitly encourages diagnosing gaps rather than only reporting outcomes:

- **Actual vs target/plan difference** is a trigger for analysis.
- **Market-share achievement** is part of the check, not only retail volume.
- External conditions (economic/political/social/environmental) may be relevant drivers, but the source says to analyze their impact; it does not authorize assuming causality.
- Dealer Review should inspect multiple domains: market, sales, stock, sales people, cash & credit.
- Strategy execution itself must be checked; poor outcome and poor execution are not automatically the same problem.
- Corrective action should be built from evaluated achievement plus field/market/competitor information.
