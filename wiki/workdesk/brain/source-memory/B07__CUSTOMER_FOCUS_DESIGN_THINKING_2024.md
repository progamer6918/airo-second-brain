# Source Dossier - Customer Focus & Design Thinking

**Source:** `Materi Day 2 - ASSDP Intermediate.pdf`  
**Scope:** 121 pages.

## Customer focus ladder

The material positions Customer Focus across production, distribution, maintenance, sales, marketing and service, then shows a value ladder:

`Satisfaction -> Loyalty -> Advocacy`.

The customer path is the 5A sequence:

`Aware -> Appeal -> Ask -> Act -> Advocate`.

## Customer needs and expectations

Five need types shown:
- Stated need.
- Real need.
- Unstated need.
- Delight need.
- Secret need.

Ways to identify needs include survey, feedback/review, 1-on-1 interview and FGD.

The source summarizes customer expectations as **CASH**:

`Convenience - Action - Speed - Hassle Free`.

## Satisfaction, delight and emotional journey

Customer Satisfaction = meeting expectations. Customer Delight = exceeding expectations.

Emotional-journey themes:
- **Joy** - sensory uplift, surprise/break-the-script, raise a normal moment into something memorable.
- **Pride** - recognize achievement/status and create a moment worth remembering.
- **Connection** - synchronize people/moment, use community/commonality, create personal closeness.

## Customer metrics

CSAT source formula:

`CSAT = (number of satisfied customers / total responses) x 100`.

The source example treats high ratings such as 4 or 5 as satisfied.

NPS classification:
- Promoter: 9-10.
- Passive: 7-8.
- Detractor: 0-6.

`NPS = %Promoter - %Detractor`.

The material also shows a conceptual "Retention Formula":

`Affinity x Satisfaction x Engagement`.

Treat this as a conceptual relationship from the training source, **not** as a validated quantitative production formula.

## Customer-centric organization

Cross-division collaboration requires unified customer experience, cross-functional communication, integrated customer feedback, coordinated problem solving and shared goals/metrics.

Customer-centric culture is connected to leadership commitment, clear vision/goals, open communication, empowerment/support and employee engagement.

## Jobs to Be Done

The source's JTBD principle: customers do not simply buy products; they "hire" something to complete a job/problem. Focus on the job/problem rather than assuming demographic attributes explain purchase behavior. Jobs are commonly written with action language such as "Bantu saya...", "Bantu saya menghindari...", or "Saya perlu...".

## Design Thinking operating model

The source's internal five-step flow is:

1. **Exploration** - immerse, observe, engage; use What/How/Why; capture behavior and context.
2. **Identification** - empathy map and synthesis into User / Need / Insight.
3. **Ideation** - generate many ideas; use "How Might We"; allow general, specific and creative options; vote using explicit criteria.
4. **Visualization** - prototype/visualize at the right fidelity, identify variables, create an experience, release failed prototypes quickly.
5. **Evaluation** - test with users, observe, ask, learn what was accepted and what remains unmet.

The process is explicitly non-linear; teams may loop back after learning.

The training also uses a 9-slide story pattern: status quo, observation, story, insight, opportunity, analogy, solution, benefit, ethos. The slide labels this "9 Slides - 5 Minutes" while each of three sub-blocks says 3 minutes; preserve the storytelling structure but do not treat duration as reliable.
