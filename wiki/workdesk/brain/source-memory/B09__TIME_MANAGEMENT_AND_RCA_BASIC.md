## Time-management system
The source's central idea is that time management is not controlling time; it is managing **self, activity and time**.

1. Clarify the roles you play.
2. Clarify the goals attached to those roles.
3. Distinguish **Important** from **Urgent** work.
4. Select weekly **Big Rocks** that materially advance roles/goals.
5. Schedule those Big Rocks before the week begins.
6. Protect execution with respect for time and integrity to the plan.

The urgent/important matrix is used to move managerial attention away from constant firefighting and toward important/non-urgent work that builds future effectiveness. Percentages shown in the training are illustrative source figures, not universal workload targets.

## Basic problem-solving sequence
1. **Problem Statement:** make the gap explicit between Current State and Future/Desired State.
2. **Problem Hypothesis:** list plausible causes, what should happen, what actually happens, and the activity/evidence needed to test each hypothesis.
3. **Root Cause Analysis:** ask why through the symptom until a controllable root cause is reached.
4. **Solution:** connect root cause to solution, PIC, deadline and status.

The training example starts from an evaluation-score gap, tests the hypothesis that delivery is too theoretical, traces it to insufficient exposure to field application, and then assigns concrete field-interaction actions. The semantic lesson is to **test causes before prescribing solutions**.
