## Semantic core

Channel Management is the deliberate management of network coverage so the channel can **increase sales by maximizing coverage** and **improve customer service, including after-sales service**. It is not just dealer-count administration. The source frames the Area Supervisor as working with Netdev to keep new/upgrade progress aligned with submitted targets, supervise progress in the field, and analyze replacement/coverage when a dealer resigns. (slides 2–3)

The failure mode the deck tries to correct is reactive management: new/upgrade realization slipping far from target, monitoring only after delays happen, and shallow analysis of dealer resignation. The desired operating model is **planned, process-level and preventive**. (slides 4–5)

## Network change has three distinct jobs

1. **New dealer** — add coverage where a new channel is needed.
2. **Replacement after resign** — diagnose why the dealer resigned and maintain area coverage through replacement and/or activity while replacement is unresolved.
3. **Upgrade dealer** — manage the full upgrade process milestone by milestone, not merely the final opening date. (slides 7–15)

For new/upgrade, the source uses a monthly target-and-monitoring form. For upgrades, monitoring runs from location finalization through approvals, layout, permits, renovation, recruitment, operational start and ODA In. The target committed to AHM is described as an estimated ODA-In timing, so schedule commitments require a process plan underneath them. (slides 8, 11–12, 15)

For resigns, the source explicitly requires monthly monitoring and **deep problem identification** plus a plan to maintain coverage in that area through replacement/activity. (slides 9–10)

## POS as an extension of coverage

The second half treats POS as a managed channel format. The 2023 guidance covers:
- storefront / identity;
- interior and dealing tables;
- unit display;
- ATL visibility;
- optional/required workshop capability by class;
- staffing and productivity;
- simple POS economics (sales, unit entry, OPEX, net profit). (slides 16–26)

The key reusable idea is not the old numeric class thresholds. It is that a POS should be designed as an **economic + service unit**: physical standard, staffing, productivity, sales capacity and profitability must fit together.

## Diagnostic / decision rules

- A missed opening date is a lagging symptom. Diagnose **which milestone slipped, why, owner/PIC, dependency and recovery path**.
- Dealer resignation is not solved by deleting a point from the map. Determine cause and preserve customer/market coverage while replacement is unresolved.
- Coverage expansion should not be measured only by number of outlets. Check whether the new/upgrade/POS actually improves reachable market, service capability and sales capacity.
- POS staffing and facility standards should be read as a system: expected volume ↔ people ↔ productivity ↔ physical capacity ↔ economics.
