## What this source teaches

This deck is not one single sales framework. It is the **human capability layer** expected of an advanced supervisor: influence, presentation, coaching, self-management, focus, critical thinking, creative thinking and innovation.

## 1. Five weapons of influence - pp.5-26

The source consistently shows **five**, not six:

1. **Social Proof** - people follow evidence that peers/others already do or endorse something.
2. **Liking** - familiarity, similarity, sincere compliments and positive connection lower resistance.
3. **Contrast** - perception changes when options/requests are deliberately compared.
4. **Time Machine** - help people imagine the future benefit/consequence.
5. **Higher Purpose** - connect the requested behavior to a bigger reason than the immediate task.

Decision rule: these are persuasion tools, not substitutes for evidence or authority. Choose the weapon based on the stakeholder barrier.

## 2. Nine-minute / nine-slide presentation - pp.27-41

The source frames a concise presentation around three psychological jobs:

- **Slides 1-3: create empathy** - Status Quo -> Observation -> Story.
- **Slides 4-6: create curiosity** - Insight -> Opportunity -> Analogy.
- **Slides 7-9: create confidence** - Solution -> Advantages -> Ethos.

The sock example is illustrative. The reusable logic is the narrative sequence.

## 3. Meeting presentation - pp.43-54

The source distinguishes public presentation from meeting presentation. Meeting communication is more data/technical and should still preserve storytelling.

Three properties recur:
- **Insightful** - move from data to meaning.
- **Confidence** - make the audience believe the recommendation is grounded and executable.
- **Simple** - one message per slide / reduce noise without oversimplifying.

## 4. Leader as Coach - pp.55-79

Coaching purpose: partnership that helps the coachee explore thinking and potential, focused on present/future.

Core competencies:
- Being Present.
- Active Listening.
- Questioning.

Questioning should favor open questions: `Apa`, `Bagaimana`, `Ceritakan`, `Jelaskan`.

GROW:
- **Goal** - desired outcome / priority.
- **Reality** - current position, obstacles, what has already been tried.
- **Options** - alternatives, resources, people who can help.
- **Will** - specific action, timing and commitment.

Session flow shown in the training:
- Opening about 15 min.
- Digging about 30 min.
- Closing about 15 min.

Opening includes preparation, relationship, confidentiality, duration, agenda and desired result. Closing shifts from exploration to action, records **1-3 agreed actions**, confirms commitment and next-session/admin follow-up.

Guard: the 15/30/15 allocation is a training format, not a universal operational SLA.

## 5. Time and focus management - pp.80-117

Key principle: **time management is self-management**, not control over time itself.

Progression:
`manage self -> manage activity -> manage time`.

Activity matrix separates:
- important + urgent;
- important + not urgent;
- not important + urgent;
- not important + not urgent.

For weekly execution:
`review roles and annual goals -> choose Big Rocks -> schedule the week`.

The deck also emphasizes:
- value your time;
- integrity to the plan;
- single-task attention;
- silence/distance phone when possible;
- disable or batch notifications;
- deep-work timer 30-45 minutes, with breaks.

An “effective manager allocation” slide shows 28% / 33% / 24% / 18%; these sum to 103%. Preserve them as source-reported illustration, **not a normative percentage target**.

## 6. Critical + Creative = Innovation - pp.118-151

The source wants an ambidextrous supervisor:
- **critical** thinking for exploitation/improvement;
- **creative** thinking for exploration/new possibilities.

Continuous improvement uses PDCA and standardization. Creative thinking uses `WHAT IF?`, analogy/association and **SCAMPER**:
- Substitute;
- Combine;
- Adopt/adapt;
- Modify/Magnify;
- Put to other use;
- Eliminate;
- Rearrange/Reverse.

Critical thinking asks `WHAT IS?` and checks:
- consistency over repeated observation;
- evidence;
- implication and realism;
- facts / what is actually true;
- repeated “why?” questioning.

Idea selection must consider **applicability and risk**.
