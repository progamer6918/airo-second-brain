## Presentation objective
The training treats presentation as an integrated art/science/skill for communicating effectively and efficiently. Three core challenges are confidence, systematic organization of the topic/idea, and making the audience remember/engage.

## Nine presentation dimensions
Across the source's framework the practical dimensions are: Readiness, Purpose, Audience, Tone/Voice, Concrete communication, Conduct/body language, Interesting delivery, Storytelling, and Enthusiasm.

Key operating rules:
- **Audience first:** focus on what the audience needs to know, not everything the presenter knows.
- Clarify purpose: instruct, inform, inspire/motivate, persuade, entertain or stimulate.
- Make abstract ideas concrete enough that the audience can mentally experience them.
- Voice control includes pronunciation, intonation, inflection, emphasis/tone, pauses and avoiding unnecessary jargon.
- Manage the emotional flow rather than presenting at one flat level.
- Use attention grabbers such as quotes, accurate statistics, questions, news, stories or acknowledgement when they fit.
- Body language includes facial expression, eye contact and purposeful hand/body movement.
- Enthusiasm transfers emotional energy; it should serve the message rather than become theatre.

## Storytelling
The source frames story as a strong communication bridge. A useful story connects a human value with the message the presenter needs the audience to retain.
