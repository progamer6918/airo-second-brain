## What My Hero is

The source defines My Hero as a **digital sales tool for developing salespeople knowledge and customer-service capability**. It centralizes product/general knowledge, product videos, articles, quiz/survey and other tools that help the frontline sell and serve with less scattered information. (slides 2, 4–5)

## Capability model

- **Product knowledge** — detailed Honda product information.
- **General knowledge** — work aids from follow-up scripts to complaint-handling tips.
- **Video & articles** — product knowledge and frequently updated information.
- **Quiz & survey** — required knowledge/qualification activities.
- **RFC (Recommended Feature Calculator)** — helps salesperson recommend a product based on known product preference or consumer attributes such as age/gender; the 2023 deck says it was included in NOS H1 Process at the MOT of asking about the unit. (slides 6–7)
- **Team Report (Kacab)** — monitors frequent login, quiz participation, quiz scores and qualified sales people. (slide 10)
- **Self Audit NOS / SENSE (Kacab)** — lets a branch head self-check NOS items and receive a Platinum/Gold/Silver/Bronze result. Critically, the source states that **Self Audit is not recognized as the official NOS audit result**. (slide 11)
- **Kamus Sosmed / Poster Komparasi / Brochure / Catalog Versus** — content and product-comparison aids intended to make frontline communication faster and more consistent. (slides 8–15)

## Management meaning

The important lesson is not the app menu itself. My Hero is a **sales-force enablement loop**:

knowledge distribution → knowledge testing → manager visibility → targeted coaching/training → better customer interaction.

A usage metric such as login rate is therefore insufficient by itself. A manager should connect utilization to knowledge quality, quiz performance, qualification and actual sales/customer execution.

## Historical usage evidence

The deck reports April 2023 national frequent-user figures and a >98% login figure later in the deck. These are historical evidence, not current utilization. (slides 2, 13)
